3. Design of Components

Our platform is client-mediated. Students and mentors each keep their own profile, but they cannot see each other. The admin (our client) looks at both sides, proposes a match and follows it until it ends.

ComponentWhat it does
Login and rolesRegistration and login for three roles: student, mentor and admin. Each role sees only its own pages.
Student moduleThe student keeps their goals, skills, wanted topics, preferred format, duration and availability, and writes in a free-text box what they need from a mentor. The student sees the status of their match, accepts or declines the mentor proposed by the admin, answers a short check-in every week, gives feedback and can raise a concern.
Mentor moduleThe mentor keeps their qualifications, topics, bio, availability slots and the maximum number of students they can mentor at the same time. The mentor writes in a free-text box what they can offer. The mentor accepts or declines match requests, sees their active mentees and answers a weekly check-in for each of them.
Admin moduleThe admin views student profiles, reviews and verifies mentor profiles, sees recommended matches, proposes a match, confirms or cancels it, changes its status, reviews the weekly check-ins that the AI flagged, and follows concerns and feedback.
Matching and recommendation (Smart Match)An AI agent gives each student and mentor pair a score out of 100 with a reason, and the admin sees the best mentors first. It only suggests. The admin decides. See section 3.1.
Mentorship PulseWeekly check-ins for both people in an active match, reminder emails, and an AI review that flags the answers that need a person. See section 3.2.
Match workflowControls the status of a match: proposed, active, completed or cancelled. A match becomes active only when the admin confirms it after both sides have accepted. Then each person receives the other's email address, and the weekly check-ins start. Every change is saved in a history.
Notifications and emailTells the mentor and the student when a match is proposed. Tells the admin when someone accepts or declines, and when a check-in is flagged. Tells both people when the admin confirms or cancels. Emails go out when a match becomes active (contact details) and when a weekly check-in is due (reminder).
DatabaseStores users, profiles, topics, mentor availability, matches with their scores, status history, weekly check-ins, AI reviews, feedback, concerns and notifications.

How a match moves: the admin proposes a match, and the mentor and the student are both notified. Each of them accepts or declines. When both have accepted, the admin can confirm the match and it becomes active. The admin can also cancel it at any time. The two people then write to each other and agree privately on session times and how they will talk. Later an active match is completed or cancelled. If someone declines, the admin cancels the match and can propose a new one.

3.1 How matches are recommended (Smart Match)

The admin should not have to compare every student with every mentor. The platform makes a short list in three steps.

StepWhat happens
1. Free textThe student writes what they need from a mentor, and the mentor writes what they can offer, in their own words (up to 800 characters). Both still choose topics from the same list.
2. Fixed rulesOnly allowed pairs are scored: the mentor is verified, the mentor has a free place (the maximum set by the mentor, 1 to 10, default 3, counts proposed and active matches), and the student has no open match.
3. AI scoreAn AI agent on the server reads the two texts, the topics and the mentor's years of experience, and returns a score from 0 to 100 and a short reason for each allowed pair. A score under 40 is shown as a weak fit.

The admin still decides. The agent only recommends. It never proposes, confirms or cancels. If the agent is unavailable, the platform uses the earlier rule score (topic overlap 70 + mentor experience 30, years counted up to 10), so the admin always has a list. The rule score is also shown next to the AI score for comparison.

Privacy. Only the two texts, the topics and the years of experience are sent to the agent. Names and email addresses are not. The profile form tells people that Smart Match reads what they write and asks them not to add phone numbers or addresses.

Example. Neha wrote that she wants to become a product manager and needs help with product requirements and interviews. Priya wrote that she mentors on product requirements, roadmaps and mock interviews. The reason shown to the admin is: "Both describe: product, manager, writing, requirements, interviews, changing; covers 2 of 2 wanted topics; 8 years of experience", with a score of 98 (rule score 94). Neha with Dr. Meera, who coaches data careers, scores 38 and is shown as a weak fit.

In the prototype the pages show this result from a simple offline stand-in that compares the wording of the two texts. It has the same inputs and outputs as the agent, so the pages do not change when the agent is connected in Sprint 2. The screens carry a note saying so.

3.2 Mentorship Pulse: weekly check-ins with human review

After a match becomes active, the platform keeps track of how it is going without the admin having to ask.

StepWhat happens
1. ContactBoth people receive an email with the other person's address. They agree session times and how they will talk (call, video, messages) between themselves. The platform does not run the sessions.
2. ScheduleOne check-in is created for each week of the mentorship, for the student and for the mentor. The number of weeks follows the chosen duration: 1 month = 4 weeks, 3 months = 12, 6 months = 24 (4 if not sure).
3. ReminderWhen a week ends, each person gets an email with a link to their Check-ins page.
4. AnswerA short form: a rating from 1 to 5 and an optional comment. The answers go to the admin only, not to the other person.
5. AI reviewAn AI agent reads each answer and gives a verdict (on track, watch or needs attention) with a short reason. It looks at the rating and the words in the comment. A rating of 2 or less is always flagged. A week without a session is not a problem by itself, because both people may agree to skip it; if something is wrong, they say so in the rating or the comment.
6. Human in the loopAn answer that is "on track" is only saved. "Watch" and "needs attention" are sent to the admin, who reads the reason, writes a note and marks it reviewed. A check-in that is still unanswered 2 days after it was due is also sent to the admin.

The agent never changes a match, cancels it or contacts anyone. It only decides what a person should look at, which keeps the admin's work small. The admin sees all of this on one page, Mentorship Pulse: what needs review, the active mentorships with their latest verdict, and the emails sent. This is how the admin follows the condition of every mentorship (US-C04), together with student feedback (US-S10) and concerns (US-S09).

In the prototype the emails are recorded in a list instead of being sent. The verdicts come from a rule-based stand-in with the same output as the agent.

Admin dashboard. The overview is kept simple. A thin line at the top shows the number of matches in each status and the number of students and mentors. The first card, "Needs your attention", lists seven things on one line each, with a count and a button that goes to the right page: matches ready to confirm, proposals declined by one side, mentors waiting to be verified, students waiting for a match, proposals waiting more than 3 days, weekly check-ins flagged for review, and open concerns. The second card shows the best mentor for each waiting student, with the Smart Match score and reason and a Propose button. The last card has four tabs, shown one at a time: open concerns, student feedback, mentor capacity and recent activity. Everything the admin needs is on this one page, and nothing is hidden. When a pair is proposed, its score and reasons are saved with the match. The server version of this logic is built in Sprint 1 (match creation APIs).

6.3 How the AI agents are called

  • The agents run on the server. The browser never holds the AI key; the key is stored as a secret setting of the backend.
  • Each agent gets a short instruction and a small JSON input, and must return a small JSON answer. Smart Match returns {score: 0-100, reason}; the review returns {verdict, reason}. The server checks the answer (score is a whole number from 0 to 100, verdict is one of the three values) and discards anything else.
  • If the agent is slow, unavailable or returns an invalid answer, the platform falls back to the rule score for matching, and to the plain rules (rating of 2 or less, or an unanswered check-in) for the review. The admin is never blocked.
  • Names and email addresses are never sent. The agent has no way to change data; the server applies the result.
  • A scheduled job runs once a day. It creates nothing new; it finds check-ins that have become due, sends the reminder emails once, and flags check-ins that are more than 2 days overdue.

9. Open Questions and Risks

  • What should happen after a mentor or a student declines? In the prototype the admin decides, cancels the match and can propose a new one.
  • Which AI provider and model do we use, who holds the key, and what is the monthly cost limit? The platform works without the agent through the fallback rules.
  • Do students and mentors agree that the text they write is read by an AI service? The profile form says so; the client should confirm the wording.
  • Which email service sends the contact and reminder emails, and which sender address is used?
  • Is one check-in per week right for every duration, and is a grace period of 2 days acceptable?
  • What should the admin do after a flag (call both people, change the match, cancel)? The prototype only records a note.
  • How long is data kept, and who may see feedback and check-in answers? Today only the admin sees check-in answers.
  • Should the system set a maximum number of students per mentor, and who keeps the list of topics?
