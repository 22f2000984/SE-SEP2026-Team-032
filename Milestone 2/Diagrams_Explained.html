How to use this guide. Read Part 1 first (the idea in plain words). Then read Part 2 (class diagram) and Part 3 (database diagram) with the picture open next to the text. Part 4 is a worked example that follows one student through the whole system. Part 5 lists review questions you can answer yes or no. You do not need to know UML or SQL beforehand; every new word is explained when it first appears.

Part 1. The idea behind both diagrams

What does our system do?

Our platform helps students of the programme get a mentor for career guidance. There are three kinds of people who use it:

PersonWhat they do on the platform
StudentRegisters, fills a profile (career goals, skills, preferences, when they are free), later sees the mentor who was assigned, and can give feedback or raise a concern.
MentorRegisters, fills a profile (qualifications, experience, areas of expertise), sets the time slots when they are free, and accepts or declines a match that was proposed to them.
Administrator (coordinator)Checks mentor profiles, proposes which mentor suits which student, and follows the progress of every match.

The one rule that shapes everything: "client-mediated"

Students and mentors cannot search or browse each other. A student cannot pick a mentor and a mentor cannot pick a student. Only the administrator makes the pairing. The pairing is called a Match. This is why you will see a Match box in the middle of both diagrams: almost everything connects to it.

Why did we choose this? It matches the problem statement (the platform acts as a middle party) and keeps control with the coordinator, who knows the students. It also means the screens are simple: a student never sees a list of mentors, only the one mentor assigned to them.

Why two diagrams?

DiagramQuestion it answersWho cares most
Class diagramWhat are the "things" in our program, what information does each hold, and what can each one do?Developers writing code
ER diagram (Entity Relationship)How do we store the information in the database tables, and how are the tables linked?Whoever builds the database and the APIs

They describe the same system from two angles, so the names are almost the same (Student, Mentor, Match, Feedback and so on). The class diagram adds actions (like "accept a match"). The ER diagram adds database details (like keys that link the tables).

Part 2. The class diagram, box by box

Look at the picture in three layers: the top (User and its three kinds), the middle (Match), and the bottom (things that belong to a Match).

Layer 1: User and its three kinds

User (marked <<abstract>>) is the common base. "Abstract" simply means we never create a plain User; every real person is a Student, a Mentor or an Administrator. User holds what every person has: an id, email, password (stored scrambled, as a hash), full name and a role. Its actions are login(), logout() and updateAccount().

The three boxes under it, joined by hollow triangles, are kinds of User. They automatically get everything User has, and add their own:

ClassExtra information it keepsActions it can do
Studentprogramme level, career goals, skills, preferences, availability note, readiness (ready or not ready to be matched)updateProfile(), viewAssignedMentor() (see the mentor chosen for them), respondToReadiness(), submitFeedback(), raiseConcern()
Mentorqualifications, years of experience, expertise areas, short bio, profile status (pending or verified)updateProfile(), setAvailability(slots), acceptMatch(m), declineMatch(m), submitFeedback()
Administratoran admin idverifyMentorProfile(), proposeMatch(s, m), changeStatus(m, status), viewOverview(), resolveConcern()

AvailabilitySlot (to the right of Mentor) is one free time window: a day of the week, a start time and an end time. The line says one Mentor has zero or many slots (1 to 0..*). A new mentor starts with zero slots and adds them later.

Notice what is missing. Student has no "search mentors" action and Mentor has no "pick student" action. Only the Administrator has proposeMatch. That is the client-mediated rule written into the diagram.

Layer 2: Match, the heart of the system

Match is one pairing of one student and one mentor. It stores a match id, the status, optional notes, when it was proposed and when the mentor responded. Its actions are the steps in its life: accept(), decline(), complete() and cancel().

The lines into Match, all with 0..* on the Match side:

  • Student to Match: one student can appear in many matches over time (for example if the first one is cancelled and a new mentor is proposed).
  • Mentor to Match: one mentor can mentor several students.
  • Administrator to Match: one administrator can propose many matches. This is the line labelled "proposes".

Layer 3: things that hang off a Match

ClassWhat it is
FeedbackA rating (1 to 5) and comments that a student or mentor gives about a match.
ConcernA problem someone raises about a match (for example "my mentor has not replied"). It has a status (open or resolved) so the administrator can follow it up.
NotificationA message sent to a user (for example "You have a new match proposal"). It remembers whether it was read.
MatchStatus (<<enumeration>>)An "enumeration" is a fixed list of allowed values. Here: proposed, active, completed, cancelled. A match can only ever hold one of these five words. It is drawn with a dashed arrow because Match uses it.

The life of a Match (status flow)

This is the most important rule for the developers and the testers. A match moves forward in these steps only. There is no separate "confirmed" step: the moment the mentor accepts, the match is active.

proposed→active→completed
Any step before completed can also go to→cancelled
FromCan go toMeaning in real life
proposedactive or cancelledThe admin suggested the pair. The mentor accepts (the match becomes active at once and the student can now see the mentor) or declines (the match is cancelled).
activecompleted or cancelledMentoring is running (the student can see the mentor). It ends normally (completed) or stops early (cancelled).
completed, cancellednothingThese are final. A finished or cancelled match is never reopened; a new match is proposed instead.

The prototype frontend already follows exactly this table, so the testers can check it by trying to make an illegal jump (for example proposed straight to completed) and confirming it is not allowed.

Part 3. The ER (database) diagram, table by table

Every box here is a table in the database. Think of each table as an Excel sheet: the field names are the column headings and each person or record is one row. Tables are linked by keys so we do not have to copy the same data twice.

Why is there a separate USER table and a STUDENT_PROFILE table?

Login details (email, password, role) are the same for everybody, so they live once in USER. The details that only students have live in STUDENT_PROFILE, and the details that only mentors have live in MENTOR_PROFILE. Each profile has a user_id that points back to its USER row. The note "UNIQUE" means one user can have only one profile. This is the database version of "a Student is a kind of User" from the class diagram.

Part 4. Worked example: one student from start to finish

Follow this story and point at the tables and classes as you go. The names are made up.

  1. Riya registers as a student. A row is added to USER (role = student) and one to STUDENT_PROFILE with her goals and skills. (Classes: User, Student.)
  2. Mr. Rao registers as a mentor. A row goes to USER (role = mentor) and MENTOR_PROFILE with profile_status = pending. He adds two free slots, which become two rows in AVAILABILITY_SLOT. (Classes: Mentor, AvailabilitySlot.)
  3. The admin verifies Mr. Rao. profile_status changes from pending to verified. (Action: verifyMentorProfile().)
  4. The admin proposes a match. The admin reads both profiles, then creates a row in MATCH with Riya's student_id, Mr. Rao's mentor_id, proposed_by = the admin and status = proposed. A row is added to MATCH_STATUS_LOG and a NOTIFICATION row is created for Mr. Rao. (Action: proposeMatch(s, m).) Riya sees nothing yet.
  5. Mr. Rao accepts. status becomes active straight away, responded_at is filled, another log row and notifications are added. Riya can now see Mr. Rao on her mentor page. (Action: acceptMatch(m).) If he had declined, status would become cancelled and the admin would propose someone else.
  6. Mentoring begins. The match stays active. Riya opens her mentor page and sees Mr. Rao. (Action: viewAssignedMentor().)
  7. During mentoring. Riya can raise a concern (a row in CONCERN, status open). The admin resolves it (status resolved). (Actions: raiseConcern(), resolveConcern().)
  8. Mentoring ends. status becomes completed. Both give feedback (rows in FEEDBACK with a rating from 1 to 5). The match is now closed for ever.

Part 5. Review checklist for Adnan

Tick each question yes or no. Write any "no" in the meeting notes with a short reason, so the team can fix it before submission.

#QuestionYes / No
1Can a student or mentor browse the other side anywhere in the diagrams? (Answer should be no.)
2Is the Administrator the only one who can propose a match?
3Does the status flow cover: propose, mentor accepts (match becomes active) or declines, complete, cancel?
4Can every field in the class diagram be found in an ER table (or is its absence explained, like Administrator)?
5Does every user story in the Milestone 1 report have a class action and a place to store its data?
6Can a student have more than one match over time (for example after a cancellation)?
7Is it clear who changed a status and when (MATCH_STATUS_LOG)?
8Are the multiplicities (1, N, 0..*) believable? Example: one mentor has many slots; one match has one student and one mentor.
9Is anything missing that the users would expect (for example the mentor's reason for declining, a calendar, chat)? Note it; do not add it without the team's approval.

Questions the team still needs to decide

  • Does a declined proposal go straight to cancelled, or do we want a separate "declined" status? (The diagrams and prototype use cancelled.)
  • Should a mentor be allowed to see the student's full profile before accepting? (The prototype shows a short summary.)
