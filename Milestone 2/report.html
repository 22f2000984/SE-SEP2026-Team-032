3. Design of Components

Our platform is client-mediated. Students and mentors each keep their own profile, but they cannot see each other. The admin (our client) looks at both sides, proposes a match and follows it until it ends.

ComponentWhat it does
Login and rolesRegistration and login for three roles: student, mentor and admin. Each role sees only its own pages.
Student moduleThe student keeps their goals, skills, wanted topics, preferred format, duration and availability. The student sees the status of their match, accepts or declines the mentor proposed by the admin, gives feedback and can raise a concern.
Mentor moduleThe mentor keeps their qualifications, topics, bio, availability slots and the maximum number of students they can mentor at the same time. The mentor accepts or declines match requests and sees their active mentees.
Admin moduleThe admin views student profiles, reviews and verifies mentor profiles, sees recommended matches, proposes a match, confirms or cancels it, changes its status, and follows concerns and feedback.
Matching and recommendationGives each student and mentor pair a score out of 100 and shows the best mentors to the admin, with reasons. It only suggests. The admin decides. See section 3.1.
Match workflowControls the status of a match: proposed, active, completed or cancelled. A match becomes active only when the admin confirms it after both sides have accepted. Every change is saved in a history.
NotificationsTells the mentor and the student when a match is proposed. Tells the admin when someone accepts or declines. Tells both people when the admin confirms or cancels, and what the next step is.
DatabaseStores users, profiles, topics, mentor availability, matches with their scores, status history, feedback, concerns and notifications.

How a match moves: the admin proposes a match, and the mentor and the student are both notified. Each of them accepts or declines. When both have accepted, the admin can confirm the match and it becomes active. The admin can also cancel it at any time. Later an active match is completed or cancelled. If someone declines, the admin cancels the match and can propose a new one.

3.1 How matches are recommended

The admin should not have to compare every student with every mentor. The platform makes a short list. The method is simple. It adds points for two facts the admin can check:

PartPointsHow it is worked out
Topic overlap70Students and mentors choose topics from the same list. The score is the share of the student's wanted topics that the mentor also covers.
Mentor experience30Years of experience, counted up to 10 years.

Pairs that are not shown: the mentor is not verified, the mentor already has as many students as the maximum they set, or the student already has an open match. Free places give no points; they only decide whether a pair is allowed.

Example. Neha wants Product Management and Career Switching. Priya covers both and has 8 years of experience, so the score is 70 + 24 = 94. Rohan wants Interview Preparation and Web Development. Priya covers one of the two (35) and Dr. Meera covers none (0). With their experience, Priya scores 59 and Dr. Meera 30, which is shown as a weak fit (below 40). Each row also shows reasons, such as "Shared topics: Interview Preparation".

Mentor capacity. Each mentor sets "maximum students at the same time" (1 to 10, default 3). Proposed and active matches use a place. A proposal that would go over the maximum is refused. A cancelled or completed match frees a place.

Admin dashboard. The top of the page has five cards with counts: matches ready to confirm, proposals declined, mentors to verify, students waiting for a match, and open concerns. Each card links to the page where the work is done. Below are the recommended matches with a Propose button, the capacity of each mentor, the number of matches by status, concerns and feedback. When a pair is proposed, its score and reasons are saved with the match. The server version of this logic is built in Sprint 1 (match creation APIs).

5. Database Design

TableWhat it stores
USEROne row for each account. The email is unique. The role is student, mentor or admin.
STUDENT_PROFILEGoals, skills, preferred format and duration, preferences and availability. One per student.
MENTOR_PROFILEQualifications, experience, bio, verification status and the maximum number of students (max_students). One per mentor.
AVAILABILITY_SLOTThe weekly time slots of a mentor. A mentor can have many.
EXPERTISE_TOPICThe shared list of topics, for example Data Science or Web Development.
STUDENT_TOPIC, MENTOR_TOPICWhich topics a student wants and which topics a mentor covers.
MATCHOne student and one mentor. It stores the admin who proposed it, the status, the mentor's and the student's answers with dates, the match score with its reasons, and the confirmation date.
MATCH_STATUS_LOGThe history of every status change: who changed it and when.
FEEDBACKThe rating and comments of a student about a match.
CONCERNA problem raised about a match. It stays open until the admin resolves it.
NOTIFICATIONMessages for users, sometimes linked to a match.

8. Open Questions and Risks

  • What should happen after a mentor or a student declines? In the prototype the admin decides, cancels the match and can propose a new one.
  • How are notifications sent, and is email needed?
  • How long is data kept, and who may see feedback?
  • Should the system set a maximum number of students per mentor, and who keeps the list of topics?
  • Does the client agree with the score weights (topics 70, experience 30)?
